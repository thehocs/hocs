Khaleda Zia, Bangladesh’s First Woman PM: A Life of Power and Resistance

2025-12-30 · 7 min read · HOCS team

As Bangladesh’s first woman Prime Minister, Khaleda Zia’s political journey is a tale of steadfast leadership amid intense challenges and enduring resistance.

Khaleda Zia emerged as a trailblazing female political figure in Bangladesh, carving out a significant role in the nation’s governance and political history. Her tenure as Prime Minister was marked by groundbreaking achievements, personal and political struggles, and a relentless fight for democracy and her party’s ideals. This article delves into her life, career milestones, and the legacy she continues to shape in Bangladeshi politics.

Early Life and Political Entry

Born on August 15, 1945, in Feni, Bangladesh, Khaleda Zia’s early life was rooted in a modest family background. She married Ziaur Rahman, a military officer who later became Bangladesh’s President. After his assassination in 1981, Khaleda entered politics, initially stepping into her husband's political shoes. Her rise was both sudden and significant, positioning her as a key player in the Bangladesh Nationalist Party (BNP).

Becoming Bangladesh’s First Woman Prime Minister

Khaleda Zia made history in 1991 when, following multi-party elections, she became the first woman to assume the office of Prime Minister in Bangladesh. Her leadership marked the beginning of a new political era with increased female representation. She held the position multiple times, leading two separate terms during which she implemented important economic and policy reforms despite facing fierce opposition.

Political Challenges and Resistance

Throughout her career, Khaleda Zia faced intense political rivalry, especially with Sheikh Hasina, leader of the Awami League. This rivalry has defined much of Bangladesh’s political landscape. Khaleda’s years in power were marked by struggles against allegations, imprisonment, and political setbacks. Nevertheless, she remained a resilient force, symbolizing resistance for her supporters.

Contributions to Bangladesh’s Development

Under Khaleda Zia’s governance, Bangladesh witnessed significant strides in infrastructure development, education, and healthcare. Her government prioritized economic liberalization and sought to attract foreign investment. Several large-scale projects, including road expansions and power plant constructions, were initiated during her tenure, contributing to the country’s modernization.

Legacy and Continuing Influence

Despite controversies, Khaleda Zia’s legacy as Bangladesh’s pioneering female leader remains influential. She broke the glass ceiling in a deeply patriarchal society, inspiring generations of women in politics. Today, she continues to lead the BNP and influences the political discourse in Bangladesh, embodying endurance amid adversity.

Conclusion

Khaleda Zia’s journey from a political widow to Bangladesh’s first female Prime Minister is a testament to her power, determination, and resilience. Her leadership reshaped the political landscape and empowered women, leaving a lasting legacy despite challenges. Bangladesh’s history and future remain intertwined with her remarkable story of resistance and influence.
