Ted Cruz Criticizes Navarro and JD Vance for Blocking India Trade Deal, Mocks Trump Tariffs in Leaked Audio

2026-01-26 · 4 min read · HOCS team

US Senator Ted Cruz has leveled strong criticism at Steve Bannon and John Henry Vance, accusing them of obstructing a significant trade agreement with India.

In leaked conversations, US President Donald Trump is heard mocking the effectiveness of tariffs. US Sen. Ted Cruz, speaking as an independent, joins in the critique against these policies.

Cruz's Accusations Against Bannon and Vance

US Senator Ted Cruz has made a series of criticisms towards Steve Bannon and John Henry Vance, two key figures within President Trump’s administration. He claims they were instrumental in preventing the United States from concluding an important trade agreement with India.

Cruz Mocks Trump's Tariffs

In a recent development, US Senator Ted Cruz has further criticized tariffs implemented by President Donald Trump. In leaked audio recordings, he mocks these policies as ineffective and counterproductive to economic growth.

The Leaked Audio Footage

A series of audio conversations have come to light that suggest Bannon and Vance were the ones behind the obstruction of the trade deal. These leaked tapes show how they attempted to thwart negotiations with India, affecting US interests in international commerce.

Cruz's Independent Perspective

Senator Cruz highlights his independent status within the Republican Party as he criticizes Bannon and Vance’s actions. His stance is a sharp departure from mainstream GOP voices who have largely supported Trump's trade policies.

Criticism Against Trump’s Tariffs Continues

In addition to criticizing Bannon and Vance, Senator Cruz has also expressed his own views on tariffs. He criticizes these measures as ineffective tools for achieving economic goals and detrimental to US industries.

Conclusion

Senator Ted Cruz's criticism of Steve Bannon and John Henry Vance highlights the internal rifts within Trump’s administration concerning trade policy. His comments not only echo public sentiments but also set a new tone in Republican Party politics regarding how they perceive Trump's trade policies.
