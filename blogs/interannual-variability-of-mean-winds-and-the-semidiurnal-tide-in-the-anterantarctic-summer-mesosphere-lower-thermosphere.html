Interannual Variability of Mean Winds and the Semidiurnal Tide in Antarctic Summer Mesosphere-Lower Thermosphere

2026-01-11 · 6 min read · HOCS team

Understanding the interannual variability of mean winds and semidiurnal tides in the Antarctic summer mesosphere-lower thermosphere is crucial for comprehending atmospheric dynamics.

The Antarctic summer mesosphere-lower thermosphere (MLT) experiences unique atmospheric conditions, with significant variations in mean wind patterns. This article examines the interannual variability of these winds and their interaction with semidiurnal tides.

Introduction to Mean Winds in the Antarctic Summer MLT

Mean winds play a critical role in shaping atmospheric circulation within the Antarctic summer mesosphere-lower thermosphere (MLT). These winds can be influenced by various factors, including solar radiation and gravitational influences.

Interannual Variability of Mean Winds in the Antarctic Summer MLT

The variability of mean winds over multiple years within this region is characterized by fluctuations in both frequency and amplitude. These changes can significantly impact mesospheric dynamics and contribute to the formation and dissipation of atmospheric waves.

Semidiurnal Tides: A Key Influence on Mean Winds

In the Antarctic summer MLT, semidiurnal tides are a significant factor affecting mean wind patterns. These tidal forces result from differential heating between day and night sides of the Earth and can induce oscillatory changes in atmospheric pressure fields.

Observational Evidence for Interannual Variability

Recent observational data indicates that the interannual variability of mean winds is correlated with semidiurnal tides. These observations reveal trends that are consistent with theoretical models and provide insights into the underlying mechanisms driving these dynamics.

Theoretical Models and Their Implications

Several theoretical models have been developed to simulate interannual variations in mean winds within the Antarctic summer MLT. These models help explain observed phenomena, such as periodic wind reversals and pressure fluctuations associated with semidiurnal tides.

Conclusion

Understanding the complex interplay between mean winds and semidiurnal tides in the Antarctic summer mesosphere-lower thermosphere is vital for improving our comprehension of atmospheric dynamics. Further research into these phenomena can contribute to more accurate modeling of climate-related processes and enhance our predictive capabilities.
